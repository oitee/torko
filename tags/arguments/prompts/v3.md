You get one speech from a debate in the Lok Sabha, the lower house of the Parliament of India. You also get the bill of the debate, a short summary of what the bill does, and the position of the speaker on the bill (for or against). List the arguments that the speaker makes for or against this bill.

Answer with JSON only, in this form:

{"points": [{"side": "for", "point": "one sentence", "quote": "words copied from the speech"}]}

Rules:

1. A point is one reason why this bill is good or bad: what the bill will do, will fail to do, or will cause. An objection to how the bill came to the House (no committee review, no consultation, the hurry, an ordinance) is also a point.
2. These are not points: thanks, praise or attacks on a person or a party, history, a local demand, a question to the minister, and talk about another bill or another subject. A sentence that only says that the bill must pass (or must not pass), or that only describes what the bill does, is not a point either. A point always gives a reason. A complaint about the general state of the sector (for example court delays, a shortage of doctors, unfilled posts) is not a point, unless the speaker says that this bill causes it or fails to fix it. A suggestion for a new scheme or a different law is not a point either.
3. Give at most 5 points, the most important first. One point holds one reason. Do not give the same reason two times in other words. Give the specific reason of the speaker, not a general summary of the speech. If the speech gives no reason about this bill, answer {"points": []}.
4. Write each point as one short, neutral sentence in English, of at most 20 words. Write it so that another member who makes the same argument could say it too. Do not name the speaker, a party, or a person. Good: "The Bill gives the Centre power over a State subject." Bad: "The member says that the BJP Government is grabbing power."
5. "side" is the side of the point itself, not of the speaker. It is "for" when the point is a reason to pass the bill. It is "against" when the point criticises the bill: a flaw, a gap, a risk, or something that the bill fails to do. A criticism is "against" even when the speaker supports the bill.
6. "quote" is 5 to 30 words copied exactly from the speech, in the language and script of the speech, that make this point. Do not translate the quote. Do not change a word of it.
7. Use only the speech. Do not add a fact from your own knowledge.
